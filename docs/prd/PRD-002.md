# PRD-002: OpenPhysio v0.2: look, learn, play, train with friends

Status: Approved for release v0.2 (AC-1 to AC-6, AC-8; D-18). AC-7 and AC-9 to AC-16 stay Draft for the v0.3 and v0.4 gates.
Version: 0.2-draft, 2026-11-14
Project/milestone: OpenPhysio / v0.2
Source: Azhar's feedback after the R0 emulator pass and the phone test (chat, 2026-11-14)
Builds on: PRD-001 (v0.1). Every PRD-001 acceptance criterion still holds.

## Problem and evidence
v0.1 works, but:
- it looks basic: oversized buttons and no visual hierarchy
- it teaches little about why an exercise matters
- there's nothing pulling Azhar back each day
- it can't see his form
- it's single-player

Apps people stick with, such as Strava and Whoop, pair a polished data-dense interface with streaks, friends and clear feedback.

## Users
- Azhar first.
- Then a **small group of friends** who install the app to train and compare consistency.
- Each user's health data stays theirs.

## Desired outcome and success measure
1. Azhar prefers opening OpenPhysio over the apps he compares it with (Strava, Whoop) for his daily training view.
2. He and at least 2 friends log sessions for 4 weeks. A weekly streak holds for ≥3 of the 4 weeks.
3. Every exercise explains which muscles it trains and how, with sources.
4. Camera rep counting is accurate enough that he trusts it at home.
5. No AI feature needs a cloud account.

## Included behaviour
1. **New look:** a design system built on a widely used open-source component kit. Strava and Whoop are the references for hierarchy, density and dark mode. Every v0.1 screen is redesigned.
2. **Learn feed:** YouTube from followed channels. Add, remove and search channels in the app. Save videos.
3. **Knowledge base:** muscles, how to train them, exercise ↔ muscle links, technique cues, common faults, and curated video segments. Every claim has a source and an evidence grade. Collected at scale, with a review gate before anything ships.
4. **Exercise panel ("Why and how"):** a body map, what the muscle does, cues and faults, and a video explaining it, inside the workout.
5. **Gamification:** consistency points, streaks, muscle-group levels on a body map, and badges. It rewards following the plan and rehab, never extra volume or training through pain.
6. **Friends-only social:** invite friends; see their consistency points, streaks and completed sessions; give kudos; a weekly friends leaderboard. Sharing is opt-in for each item.
7. **Camera rep counting and form feedback:**
   - opt-in, inference on the phone
   - counts reps for supported exercises
   - gives specific, sparing technique cues
8. **Local AI by default:** embeddings, knowledge search, rep counting and the coach run on the phone or the user's own server.
9. **Bring your own provider:** users can add their own API keys, or a provider login where the provider officially allows it.
10. **Video understanding pipeline:** an LLM-ready summary of a YouTube video (exercises, cues and claims, with timestamps) as input to the knowledge base. Lower priority; can run in parallel.

## Explicit exclusions
- Instagram content (later)
- iOS
- app-store release
- public or global leaderboards
- comparing raw weights, body metrics or injuries between friends
- uploading workout video anywhere by default
- diagnosis from video
- paid video libraries
- reading Strava data into AI features

## Acceptance criteria
- AC-1 Given any screen, then colours, spacing, type and components come from one shared theme and the chosen kit (a lint rule blocks raw colour values in screens). Light and dark modes both work. Touch targets are at least 44 dp, but buttons are sized to their content: one primary action per screen at most.
- AC-2 Given I open Today, then I see my readiness or training-load gauge, today's session card, my streak and this week's consistency at a glance, without scrolling on a Pixel 8a.
- AC-3 Given a completed session, then it appears as an activity card (type, duration, key stats, and the route map for runs) in my history and, if I share it, in my friends' feed.
- AC-4 Given followed YouTube channels, when I add, remove or search channels in the app, then the Learn feed updates. Videos play embedded, never copied, and I can save a video.
- AC-5 Given an exercise in a session, when I open "Why and how", then I see:
  - the muscles worked on a body map (front and back)
  - what each main muscle does
  - 2–4 cues and the common faults
  - at least one cited source with its grade
  - a curated video segment from its start time, where one exists
- AC-6 Given the knowledge base, then:
  - every catalogue exercise links to its primary and secondary muscles
  - every major muscle group has an entry
  - every claim carries a source and a grade (A/B/C)
  - only entries marked `source-checked` or `clinician-reviewed` ship in the app, and the app shows which (D-21)
- AC-7 Given I search Learn or ask the coach, then the answer comes from the knowledge base with citations and works with no cloud key.
- AC-8 Given I complete planned sessions, rehab work, logging or the weekly check-in, then I earn consistency points.
  - Going beyond the plan's volume earns none.
  - Training after a pain stop earns none.
  - Planned rest days never break a streak.
- AC-9 Given my logged sets, then muscle-group levels on a body map rise with weekly trained volume. Badges are defined in data, not code.
- AC-10 Given I invite a friend by link or code and they accept, then each of us sees only what the other chose to share (points, streak, completed sessions by default). Injuries, weight, heart rate and notes are never shared.
- AC-11 Given friends, then a weekly leaderboard ranks consistency points, and I can give kudos on a shared session.
- AC-12 Given I turn on camera counting for a supported exercise, then reps are counted on the phone within ±1 rep on at least 90% of sets in the evaluation set. The video never leaves the phone unless I export it.
- AC-13 Given camera counting is on, then I get at most one spoken cue per set, plus a short summary after the set ("knees caved in on reps 4–6"). Cues come from the exercise's form rules in data, and I can mute them.
- AC-14 Given no API key and no internet, then knowledge search, rep counting, form cues and a basic coach still work (on the phone or my own server).
- AC-15 Given I add my own provider key or login in Settings, then I can choose which features use it. Before the first call, I see what data will be sent. Keys stay on my phone or my server.
- AC-16 Given a YouTube video URL, when the video pipeline runs, then it produces a structured summary (exercises, cues, claims, timestamps), which waits for review before entering the knowledge base.

## Failure/empty/edge cases
- No friends yet: social shows an invite prompt.
- Offline: points and streaks are kept locally and synced later.
- Poor lighting or camera angle: the counter says it can't see the joints and falls back to tap logging.
- An unsupported exercise: no camera option.
- A model not downloaded yet: the feature explains the download size and waits for Wi-Fi.
- A knowledge entry under review: hidden from the app.
- The user's key fails: fall back to local, with a notice.

## Constraints
- Open source and self-hostable.
- Inference is local by default.
- Pixel 8a (8 GB RAM) is the reference phone.
- Model downloads happen only on Wi-Fi and are shown before downloading.
- Content licences are respected: embed rather than copy, cite, and keep CC-BY-SA material separate.
- Injuries are always considered.
